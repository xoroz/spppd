# SPPPD

A top-down racing game built with [LÖVE](https://love2d.org) 11.5. The handling is
built on a tyre and vehicle model, tuned for arcade fun by default. Everything you
see and hear is generated by the game, and freestyle will play your own music.

Its website, with downloads, the changelog and a blog, is
<https://spppd.xyz>.

    make run

Race the AI, set time trials, play in freestyle's lot of cones and containers or
cruise any circuit with no clock, build your own tracks, or race up to three
friends online. There are 16 cars, 12 tracks (two of them real circuits, Mugello and Barcelona) and four
times of day. It also runs in a web browser.

## Controls

| Action | Keys | Pad (Xbox / PlayStation) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Steer | A D / arrows | Left stick |
| Accelerate, brake and reverse | W S / arrows | RT LT / R2 L2 |
| Handbrake (drift on Arcade) | Shift | RB / R1 |
| Boost on/off | Space | LB / L1 |
| Reset to the track | R | Y / Triangle |
| Headlights (low, high) | F, G | |
| Change camera | C | Back / Share |
| Shift gears in manual | Q, E | X A / Square Cross |
| Next song in freestyle | N | D-pad right |
| Horn | H | B / Circle |
| Pause | Esc or P | Start / Options |

These are the defaults. CONTROLS > KEYBOARD and CONTROLS > PAD change them: pick an
action, then press its new key or button. The arrows, right Shift and Esc always work
as above, the stick always steers, and the triggers always give gas and brake, though
a pad button can be set for those too.

At the start lights, hold the revs on the white bar for a good launch.

**Touch** controls appear on phones and tablets: a stick under your left thumb, DRIFT
and BOOST on the right, and horn, reset and pause at the top right. The car accelerates
by itself unless you turn Auto Gas off. With buttons steering and Auto Gas off, GAS and
BRAKE sit side by side and one thumb slides between them. The manual gearbox puts DOWN
on the left and UP on the right, like paddles.

**A pad works on a phone too.** Press anything on it, even just the stick, and the touch
controls get out of the way. Touch the screen to bring them back. If the pad drops out
mid-race, the race pauses.

**Two players at one screen** on a desktop: plug in a pad and set PLAYERS to 2 in race
or freestyle setup. The screen splits down the middle. With one pad, P1 drives on the
keyboard and P2 on the pad; with two, P1 can use the keyboard or the first pad. Each
player picks their own car, paint, handling and gearbox.

**CONTROLS** in the main menu holds all of this: touch controls on, off or automatic,
stick or buttons steering, Auto Gas, the gearbox, the size and opacity of the touch
controls, vibration, and the KEYBOARD and PAD screens that change keys and buttons. On a
phone, BUTTON LIST shows the touch controls.

## Good to know

- **Fences** bite: a hard hit wrecks the car, which costs you over a second and all your
  speed. Glancing off one is fine, and so is being shoved into one by another car.
- **Handling** is set in race setup. Arcade is the default, with big grip and a held
  drift on the handbrake. Easy is Arcade with 50% more grip on every car, while the
  computer drivers stay on Arcade. Sport adds realistic grip with driver aids, and Sim has no
  aids at all. Online races always use Arcade.
- **Boost** fills on its own, and three times faster while the car is sliding.
- **Slipstream**: sit close behind a car at speed for less drag, then pull out and
  slingshot past.
- **Pace notes** at the top of the screen show the next corner, how tight it is (1 is a
  hairpin, 4 a fast kink), how far it turns (90°, a 180° hairpin) and when to brake.
- **QUICK RACE**, first in the main menu, starts the race you set up last in one press.
  Race setup opens on START, so RACE then START works as well.
- **Radio**: freestyle plays MP3, OGG, FLAC or WAV files. Drop them on the game window,
  or use MUSIC FOLDER in freestyle setup. Not on phones.

| MUSCLE MONSTER | Supercharged 8-cyl | RWD | 601 kW (806 hp) | 1980 kg | 3.4 s | 8.6 s | 340 km/h |
| CYBER | Tri-motor electric | AWD | 774 kW (1038 hp) | 2160 kg | 2.4 s | 6.4 s | 328 km/h |
| TOFU RUNNER | 4-cyl | RWD | 175 kW (235 hp) | 1040 kg | 4.0 s | 15.5 s | 259 km/h |
| WEDGE | 12-cyl, mid-engined | RWD | 435 kW (584 hp) | 1520 kg | 3.0 s | 8.7 s | 331 km/h |
| QUAD TURBO | Quad-turbo 16-cyl | AWD | 683 kW (916 hp) | 1950 kg | 2.2 s | 6.5 s | 381 km/h |
| BAJA BEAST | Supercharged 8-cyl | AWD | 466 kW (625 hp) | 2900 kg | 3.9 s | 15.1 s | 276 km/h |
| OPEN WHEELER | Turbo 6-cyl | RWD | 502 kW (673 hp) | 920 kg | 3.1 s | 6.5 s | 338 km/h |

The times and speeds come from the game's own physics on Arcade handling: full throttle
from a standstill, without boost. A good launch at the lights takes about half a second
off. Sport handling has less grip, so most cars are slower off the line there.
Up to 100 km/h every car is limited by its tyres, not its engine, so all-wheel drive and
weight over the driven wheels count most. Power shows after that: OPEN WHEELER is only
mid-pack to 100 km/h, but with the best power to weight here it's among the quickest to 200.
Four more came after these: GT RACER (a winged endurance coupe, front V8), RALLY KING
(a four-wheel-drive turbo hatch that shrugs off gravel), PROTOTYPE (a light 24-hour car
with big downforce) and DRIFT KING (a big-turbo rear-drive saloon on hard tyres, with
extra steering lock). Then TATUS, a junior single-seater: the grippiest tyres in the
garage, middling handling and the lowest top speed.

Race setup shows each car's SPEED, ACCEL, BRAKE, HANDLING and TIRES, measured from the
game's own physics on Sport handling.

Raw numbers aren't the whole story: corners decide most laps, and TOFU RUNNER and 4WD
MONSTER corner harder than MUSCLE MONSTER or BIG BULL.

## Desktop and phone

The game is the same on both, with the same cars, tracks and physics, and phones race
desktops online. What differs:

| | Desktop (Windows, Linux) | Phone (Android) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Race, time trial, freestyle | Yes | Yes |
| Online, two to four players | Yes | Yes |
| Two players at one screen | Yes, with a pad for the second | No |
| Track editor | Yes | No |
| Your own tracks | Make and race them | Race them online, from the host |
| Freestyle radio, your own music | Yes | No |
| Controls | Keyboard, pad, or touch on a touchscreen | Touch stick or buttons, or a pad |
| Auto gas | With touch controls | On by default, can be turned off |
| Manual gearbox | Yes | Yes, with UP and DOWN buttons |
| Headlights | Low or high beam | High beam only |
| Camera | Switch while driving | In SETTINGS, or Share on a pad |
| Vibration | Pad rumble, road and hits | Phone buzz on hits |
| Fullscreen | Optional | Always |

## Track editor

Open it from the main menu, on a desktop. Click to add a node, drag to move it, right
click to delete it, and select one to change its width. Press **T** to drive the track
at any time. Kerbs, barriers and the racing line are worked out for you.

## Online

Two to four players race through a relay server. One player picks HOST A ROOM, sets the
track, laps and time of day, and reads out the room code. The others enter it and join,
and only the host needs the track. REMATCH runs the race again once everyone asks.

Everyone needs the same version of the game. The ONLINE screen lists what else to
expect: it's new, you can't rejoin after leaving, and nobody checks results.

What the official server keeps is in [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md), and its rules are in
[TERMS.md](TERMS.md). PRIVACY & TERMS on the ONLINE screen opens them.

To run your own relay, open UDP port 8080 and have everyone set SERVER to your address:

    make server
    ./dist/relay -addr :8080 -dashboard 127.0.0.1:9090

The dashboard at <http://localhost:9090> shows rooms and traffic, and can close rooms
and ban IPs. Bans and dashboard users are kept in `spppd-relay.db` (`-db`). Until you
add a user, the dashboard only answers on this machine; with one, it takes a login from
anywhere:

    ./dist/relay user add NAME

`-max-players` (2000) and `-max-rooms` (no limit) cap how many people and rooms the
relay takes at once, and the dashboard shows both against their limits.

Statistics are off unless you start the relay with `-analytics`. They count unique IPs
as keyed hashes, plus rooms, races and traffic, and the dashboard exports them as CSV.
If you turn them on, tell your players, as `PRIVACY.md` does for the official server.
`-status :8081` serves how many drivers are online and races running as JSON at
`/api/status`, which <https://spppd.xyz/status/> shows for the official server.

**In a browser** (`./build.sh`, then serve `dist/web/` over HTTP, e.g.
`python3 -m http.server -d dist/web 8020`) the game is the desktop one without online
play, the radio or QUIT. On a phone's browser it plays like the phone build, and Auto Gas
starts off there, so GAS and BRAKE buttons sit beside the stick. `update.sh` is the
server's cron hook: it rebuilds and publishes `dist/web/` when `master` moves.

`make deploy-relay` deploys the official server, and `make check-relay` checks it answers
without showing in its stats. See `ARCHITECTURE.md`.

## Building and testing

    make test                 # simulation, rules, editor, audio, about 8 minutes
    make test-visual          # the real game through its input handlers
    make test-server          # the relay
    make test-online          # clients through a real relay
    make test-online-visual   # the real game online, against bots
    make zip win64 win32      # release builds
    ./build.sh                # the .love, then the browser build in dist/web/ (love.js, needs npx)
    make apk                  # a test build for Android
    make site                 # the website, after the release builds
    make deploy-site          # the website, deployed to spppd.xyz
    make itch                 # the release builds, uploaded to itch.io

`ARCHITECTURE.md` explains how the game works, `SOUND.md` how it's meant to sound,
and `ROADMAP.md` what's next.

## Browser build (apps.texngo.it)

A browser copy runs at <https://apps.texngo.it/spppd/>, built with
[love.js](https://github.com/Davidobot/love.js) 11.4.1 by `texngo/build-web.sh`. Online play
is not available there: browsers cannot open UDP sockets. Everything else works.

`texngo/build-web.sh` runs `./build.sh`, the same browser build as above, so the page gets
`web/bridge.js`: the game fills the browser window, a phone gets the touch controls, and
clicks land where they're aimed. The game copes with the browser's plain Lua 5.1 itself (no
sockets, no `bit`, no yield across `pcall`, no mipmaps on odd-sized textures).

`texngo/update.sh` runs from cron every 10 minutes: when `master` has new commits it pulls,
rebuilds into `dist/web.new` and swaps it in only if the build succeeds. Details, including
the Apache alias and the Pangolin route, are in `texngo/README.md`.
